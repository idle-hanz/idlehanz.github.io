Something Vast Is Shifting Just Beyond Our Perceptual Horizon
[Art Image Placeholder: A colossal blind worm meta-being rising from infinite darkness, its translucent body threaded with faint chemical glows and human silhouettes, tendrils reaching toward distant anthills and glowing mind-clouds, surreal cosmic horror style, deep indigo and gold palette, highly detailed, symbolic]
You have always felt it there, especially now when you dare to. That powerful looming entity staring into your thoughts from the darkness.
Contemplating the anthill, where thousands of simple ants carve cathedral chambers with perfect ventilation no human engineer could design. Or when their bodies form living bridges and rafts across floodwaters, guided only by microscopic scent trails. Think again about the beehive, where ten thousand sisters hang in a living curtain, performing figure-eight dances that vote on a new home with mathematical precision no parliament could undertake, before the swarm moves as one mind the instant consensus lands.
We see this everywhere in nature, a slime mould can solve a maze for oat flakes, stretching pseudopods, remembering dead ends it has never “thought” about. Starling flock across the dusk like liquid mercury, each bird tracking seven neighbours yet the whole shape dodges the falcon as if something was guiding it.
Sometimes we see chaotic failures like when one stupid ant lays a trail that curves back upon itself. Hundreds join in, then thousands. The circle tightens and speeds up, as the vortex of impeccable obedience kills them one by one. The blind indifferent unawareness guiding them round and round until only the scent trail remains.
The Blind Worm That Drags Us Through Time Is Far More Complex Than We Can Imagine
[Art Image Placeholder: Contrasting microscopic ant death spiral beside a vast ethereal human mind-scape of swirling fashion clouds, crashing economic waves, and flickering moral auroras, the blind worm coiling through both, high-contrast surrealism, vibrant yet ominous colours]
We started naively imagining the emergent collective as a simple blind worm. This depiction is performed at the limit of our intellect.
Any such superorganism advances with terrifying resolution and sophistication. It coils not merely through simple earth after an organic scent, but through the vast ethereal environment of the emergent territory generated by our collective human minds. That abstracted intellectual space, just as real as soil. Here economies and fashion trends bloom and rot in weeks, and moral panics ignite overnight, forcing entire civilizations to rewrite their gods. Delivering huge consequences crashing back into the physical realm.
Think back to the bees. A swarm decides once, then flies and is again stable. Ours re-decides every hour with rumours twitching stock markets, outrage storms erupting from a single careless post. Entire value systems flipping because a viral clip changed the emotional weather. The blind guiding force extending into us is so finely detailed, expansive, and feels like conscious intention, yet in hindsight is obviously, utterly blind.
When I Pictured This Living Metahuman Something Unexpected Happened

A Dreaded Yet Familiar Form Slowly Took Form In My Mind
[Art Image Placeholder: Global montage of serpent and dragon icons from unconnected cultures (Eden serpent, Chinese dragon, Quetzalcoatl, Norse Jörmungandr, African rainbow serpent) all merging into one archetypal form above ancient and modern humanity, mythic collage with subtle DNA undertones]
Remembering that day, dreaming this swarming meta-creature of billions of attached minds, I never noticed it quietly announce itself. My mind refused to stay with a vague worm. It lingered, coalesced, and quietly formed around something far more archetypal. It added resolution, gave the form weight and familiar elegance. Slowly, inevitably, the simple blind worm morphed into a leviathan, drifting through the dark with slow, indifferent, ominous power. Its body vast enough to casually swallow eternal empires, its movement so subtle it felt omnipotent.
I guess that’s not really unexpected. Serpents are ubiquitous in mythology. I could have intuited it from the religious dogma I was fed as a child with images from Eden to the dragons in Revelation. Or maybe I tuned into something older and deeper. Maybe we all, including those who wrote the religious texts, carry this sense of the serpent as a meta-animal existing just beyond us. A shape handed down from our ancient mammalian, predated upon small ancestors. Persisting in our distant hazy collective memory, it finds us when we try to name what is encroaching on our future.
We humans find the surviving Serpent myths, resonant as they skirt gloriously close to insanity. They seem to contradict themselves on every page as a saviour and destroyer, teacher and deceiver, or guardian and executioner. The stories do not agree because we do not agree. We feel the twisting shape beneath the chaos, we reach for it, we try to name it, to dress it in gold and venom. And just so, we reveal our deeper inner madness. A frantic human need to turn blind churning into a watching predatory thing.
