### Revised style prompt (assistant — latest full draft in this thread)

```

Phrygian dominant mode tension in verses for serpentine unease; chorus evolves into chromatic descending harmonic minor for tragic falling emotion. Peter Murphy-style dramatic baritone vocals — intimate and controlled in verses with cracked vulnerability, then exploding into passionate, unhinged, raw intensity in choruses with layered detuned echoes, vocal cracks, and theatrical screams. Prioritise emotional weight and rhythmic precision over perfect rhyme.
```

### Assistant diagnostic (this thread, not book text)

Strengths named: 5/4 gothic ritual frame; hypnotic unease; Peter Murphy theatricality; core images (ape crown of mud, mirror weeping, ordinary men pulling triggers).

Weak spots named:
- Clunky / slightly purple lines: “the fire that will never stop”, “pathetic throne”.
- Chorus repeats “Ape's illusion” four times — impact drops after the second.
- Serpent appears too early and too watching-god-like for a Ch1-aligned piece (risk of mythologising before the Ch3 snap).
- 5/4 needs tighter syllable counts.
- Emotional peak arrives too early in the final chorus; fade needs more chill/humility residue.

Target stated: keep raw self-implicating voice; tighten cadence and sensory weight; delay/soften Serpent to a faint witness (poetic, not explanatory); make final chorus the true destabilisation peak, then drop into quiet resignation.
